SECTION A · LEAD · DURHAM, NC

Healthcare ops, quietly automated.

A four-year-old startup deploys AI employees inside the same browsers, fax lines, and phone trees clinicians use — and produces, by some measures, more work than teams five times its size.

By The Record Staff Filed 09:14 EDT · Updated continuously
Fig. 1 — Schematic of GenHealth's operational flow across web, fax, and voice channels.

The administrative work behind a single CPAP claim — a fax to read, a policy to apply, a phone call to make, an 835 to post — used to occupy four people over the course of two weeks. At GenHealth, it now occupies four agents over the course of twenty-one days. None of them are people.

The Durham, NC company, founded in 2023, deploys what it calls "AI employees" inside the very systems clinicians and billers already use: Brightree, Epic, athenahealth, UnitedHealthcare's portal, the supplier's fax line, RingCentral. The agents do the work the human team had been doing — intake, eligibility, prior authorization, claims, denials, posting — only continuously, and with a written record of every action.

"More work, with better results, than a team five times their size."— Jack Bachman, Piedmont Medical Solutions

The pitch is plain enough that customers describe it as a relief. Where prior tools promised to summarize documents or suggest codes, GenHealth's agents finish the task: a Certificate of Medical Necessity is read and the intake record is written. A prior authorization is adjudicated against payer policy and the four chart citations are attached. A 1992-day's worth of resupply calls go out before lunch.

Every action is logged. Every decision cites the policy line and the chart note that drove it. The result is a quieter kind of automation — one customers say they trust because they can read it.

"We catch problems sooner now," Bachman said in a written statement to The Record. "And we have a level of transparency we never had before."
