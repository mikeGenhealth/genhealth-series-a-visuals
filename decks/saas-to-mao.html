The problem

Stage 2 · MAP 06 / 14

Stage 2 · Managed Agent Provider (MAP)

We operate the agents.
Customers buy outcomes.

  • The model. GenHealth stands up and runs the agents inside the customer's workflow. Customer sees SLAs, throughput, and outcomes — not agent configs.
  • The pricing. Per-transaction or % of throughput (claims processed, PAs adjudicated, orders intaken). Moves the line item from software budget to operations budget.
  • The TAM unlock. BPO/MSP spend is 5–10× the size of the software budget we sell into today — $296B and growing to $626B by 2032.
  • What we need to stand it up. Operations layer with 24/7 coverage · SLA tooling · agent governance + observability · customer-facing ops leaders. Not a team of 3,000; a team of 30–50.
  • First pilot. Convert TBD existing customer from seat pricing to outcomes pricing. Target: same revenue at higher run-rate, expanding from TBD workflows to full admin stack.

Stage 3 · MAO 07 / 14

Stage 3 · Managed Agent Organization (MAO)

The MSO playbook —
rebuilt with SaaS cost structure.

Contract directly with health plans for specific admin functions: prior auth, claims, eligibility, medical-necessity review. Revenue as % of collections, outcome-based, eventually at-risk.

Dimension MSO MAO · Managed Agent Organization
Who does the work Large teams of billers, coders, coordinators AI agents with a small ops team supervising
How you grow Hire more people, acquire more groups Sign more contracts, deploy more agents
Revenue model 10–25% management fee on practice revenue % of collections or per-transaction on claims processed
Gross margins 15–30% (labor-heavy) 60–85% (software-like cost structure)
Onboarding speed 60–120 days to stand up a new group Days to weeks — configure agents, connect data
Payer relationship Negotiates contracts for the provider group Contracts directly with health plans for specific functions
Defensibility Payer contracts, regional density, physician ties Proprietary agents, workflow IP, claims training data

An MSO scales by hiring; a MAO scales by deploying agents — same revenue lines on the P&L, radically different cost structure underneath.

Margin Profile 12 / 14

Margin profile

Software margins on
services-size revenue.

MSOs and BPOs fight for 15–30% gross margins because their cost of goods sold is labor. Ours is compute and a small ops team. Margin compresses modestly as we move down-stack; the revenue line does not.

Stage Revenue model Cost of goods Gross margin Revenue / contract
Stage 1 · SaaS Per-seat / per-workflow Compute + customer success 70–80% $60K ADV
Stage 2 · MAP Per-transaction / % throughput Compute + ops supervisors (1 : 50+ agents) 60–70% $500K–$5M
Stage 3 · MAO % of collections / at-risk Compute + compliance + actuarial 50–70% $10M–$100M+
Reference · MSO Mgmt fee on revenue Billers, coders, coordinators 15–30% $5M–$50M
Reference · BPO FTE billable / % collections Offshore labor stack 20–35% $1M–$10M

Why the margin holds: agents' cost-per-transaction scales sublinearly with volume. A 10× increase in throughput drives ~2× increase in compute cost. The operating team grows from 30 at Stage 2 to ~150 at Stage 3 — still 1–2 orders of magnitude smaller than the equivalent MSO.
